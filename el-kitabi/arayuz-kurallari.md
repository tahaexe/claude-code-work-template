# UI visual rules and traps

## Rules
- **Palette:** per game one brand hue, one reward gold, one primary-action colour, one danger colour (destructive actions only), 2-3 tinted neutrals. No pure #000 or flat #808080 surfaces; dark themes in deep navy, plum or dark brown. Keep the contrast hierarchy (pieces > board > background > HUD). Meaningful colours pass renk-korlugu-palet.md (shape + colour).
- **Type:** one display family (chunky rounded: Nunito ExtraBold, Lilita One, or the pack font rebuilt with Turkish glyphs) for titles, numbers and buttons; body in the same or one readable family; 3-4 sizes per screen. Phone minimums: body ≥ 14 dp, button labels ≥ 18 dp, titles ≥ 24 dp, nothing < 12 dp (legal lines ≥ 11); prefer 16-17 dp body (Apple's default body is 17 pt), larger for older audiences. Xbox's XAG 101 asks far more for console UIs streamed to phones (18 px at 100 DPI ≈ 29 dp body height): not our floor, but a game with a lot of reading offers a text-size option, and the App Store's Larger Text label needs 200%. Blocks over two lines: line spacing ≥ 1.5, sentence case (one- or two-word labels exempt). Display text: light fill, dark outline (8-12% of the size), soft drop shadow. Inspector sizes are canvas units: with ScaleWithScreenSize, a portrait reference width W and match = width, 1 dp ≈ W/360 units on the strict profiles; for W = 1080, body 14 dp ≈ 42 units, button 18 dp ≈ 54, title 24 dp ≈ 72. The test (testleri #3) has the final word.
- **No TMP UpperCase, LowerCase or SmallCaps style on localized labels:** TMP's case styles follow the thread's `CurrentCulture` (the phone's), not the game's language (Unity Localization's locale); Unity files it as a feature request, not a bug (TMPB-188). Turkish loses the dotted İ on English phones and English shows "SETTİNGS" on Turkish phones. Uppercase the string in code with the language's CultureInfo (`new CultureInfo("tr-TR")`, `CultureInfo.InvariantCulture` for English), or write it uppercase in the string table. Check GUI Pro prefabs too: their labels may carry the style.
- **Buttons:** three layers (body, top gloss, bottom lip) and a dark outline. One primary per screen: the largest (area ≥ 1.3× the next interactable), most saturated, in the thumb zone. Secondary smaller or desaturated; tertiary as icon or text. Role = colour + icon, never colour alone: rewarded = video icon + number, purchase = store price, close = ×, destructive = red. Pressed: scale 0.92-0.96 in the same frame + click sound; disabled: 40-50% alpha or desaturated, still readable.
- **Touch:** ≥ 48 dp hit area on every profile (Android's 48 dp, stricter than Apple's 44 pt: one rule for all); gaps ≥ 8 dp (Apple suggests ~12 pt around bezelled and ~24 pt around borderless controls). Enlarge the hit area, not the art: a negative Raycast Padding on the art's Image (zero code; GraphicRaycaster honours it), or a non-drawing Graphic (Project F's `RaycastTarget`: `OnPopulateMesh` → `vh.Clear()`). uGUI's `RaycastReceiver` (6000.5+) and `SafeArea` component (6000.6+) don't exist on our 6000.3.
- **Revenue buttons act once:** Continue, Purchase, Rewarded and Claim ignore a second tap until their action resolves (non-interactable meanwhile), so a fast double tap never spends or grants twice.
- **Android back** (Escape in the Input System) closes only the top popup; it never quits the game from a popup or mid-level.
- **Tablets keep physical size:** in uGUI, set the CanvasScaler match to height (1) when the aspect ratio is below 1.6, centre the button groups and cap widths with LayoutElement.
- **Safe area:** buttons, text and HUD inside the safe area; backgrounds and dimmers full-bleed. Android 15+ (targetSdk 35) is edge-to-edge: status bar, cut-out and gesture bar overlap the app. Layout code reads one screen provider, never `Screen.safeArea`/`Screen.width`/`Screen.dpi` directly (the tests inject profiles through it).
  - Full-screen layers (dimmer, popup root, full-screen background) live outside the safe-area container; inside it, a notched phone shows an undimmed strip under the status bar (a quiz game's info popup, 2026-10-17).
  - Inside the container, proportional anchors (e.g. `anchorMax.y` 0.79) shrink as the safe area shrinks while fixed-offset bars stay put: a list under a fixed top bar anchors to the bar (`anchorMax.y = 1` and a fixed `offsetMax.y`), or it slides under it on tall-notch phones.
- **Nothing under fixed bars:** a scrolling list's last item scrolls fully clear of a fixed bottom bar or banner ad (bottom padding = bar height + safe-area inset); nothing tappable sits under a banner.
- **Drag has a tap path:** a core action done by dragging also works by tapping (tap to pick, tap to place), or the design notes say why not; older and one-handed players miss drags. `EventSystem.pixelDragThreshold` counts pixels: set it from dp through the screen provider, so a tap on a dense phone isn't read as a drag.
- **Dialog surface:** ≥ 1.3:1 lighter than the dimmed scene, or a light rim; a dark outline vanishes on a dark dimmer (Project M's card was 1.10:1).
- **Two languages:** design for the longer one (Turkish often +20-40% on short labels); auto-size only with a minimum that is itself ≥ the type minimum; wrap or rephrase before shrinking. Casing in code with the language's culture (above). Turkish has no plural after a number ("5 elma", never "5 elmalar"): one template per language, never English "s" logic; Smart String plural rules only serve English one/other. tr-TR numbers: thousands dot, decimal comma (1.250,5); counters, timers and scores formatted with the language's CultureInfo, not the phone's. Every glyph comes from the label's font or its explicit fallbacks (no GUI Pro font has ğ ı ş İ Ğ Ş: layerlab-gui-pro.md). Store prices arrive in the storefront's currency (€, ₺, R$, ₹, ¥, ₩) whatever the game language: the price font needs those glyphs or an ISO-code fallback (Project F's ShopView).
- **Accessibility labels (App Store):** these rules let a game honestly declare Sufficient Contrast (text 4.5:1, controls 3:1 by default), Reduced Motion (oyun-hissi.md), Differentiate Without Color Alone (shape + colour) and Dark Interface (if dark by default); Larger Text needs text at ≥ 200% (or the system's largest size) without overlap. Declare only what holds for every common task (app-store-game-center.md).
- **Cute and polished:** rounded corners (panels ≥ 12 dp radius; Image.pixelsPerUnitMultiplier sets the 9-slice corner size), soft gradients and inner highlights, gentle shadows, one icon family, a mascot or the game's objects reacting to wins and losses, particles on rewards. Dark or gritty games keep the darkness in the scene; the UI stays friendly.
- **Small polish that shows:**
  - Changing numbers (score, coins, timer, price) use fixed-width digits, or the label jitters as they change: a font with tabular digits, or TMP's `<mspace=…em>` tag around the number (every character takes the same advance; size it to the widest digit and check a capture). The tag widens separators too, so formatted numbers (1.250,5) look gappy with it: prefer a tabular-digit font for them.
  - Nested corners: a rounded surface inside a rounded panel takes outer radius = inner radius + the gap between them; equal radii leave a wider gap at the corners than along the sides.
  - Asymmetric icons (play triangle, arrows, speech bubbles) are centred by eye, not by their box: a play triangle sits slightly right of centre. Judge on a capture.

## Traps
- **Linear colour space:** semi-transparent dimmers look lighter than their alpha suggests, because blending happens in linear light (an sRGB target is linearized, blended and re-encoded). A design tool's 75% black veil is not alpha 0.75 in Unity: pick the alpha for the darkening wanted on screen, about 0.91 for 70%, 0.93 for 75%, 0.96 for 82% on mid-tones (0.85 reads ~60%), then measure 1 − shown/base outside the panel on a screenshot (Project B needed 95%, Project M 75%). Baked shadows, glows and soft sprite edges likely look lighter and thinner than in the mock-up too (inferred from Unity's linear-workflow notes): check them on captures and raise their alpha if needed; leave the texture import settings alone. `Canvas.vertexColorAlwaysGammaSpace` only improves the precision of dark colours; it doesn't change blending. Scripts that predict a blended colour (contrast, colour-blind checks) must blend in linear light (sRGB → linear, mix, back) and confirm with a screenshot pixel: white at 35% over a navy sky is #6A6F8D blended in sRGB but #A1A2AA on screen (a colour gap of 28.1 believed, 21.4 real). Gold blended into navy turns muddy brownish grey (#655553): gold halos and glints look dirty on dark grounds.
- **A tap that closes a panel must not reach the board:** queuing game taps at press time and handling them in `Update` (even with the root at `[DefaultExecutionOrder(-100)]`) let the tap that closed the settings panel also play a piece on the board, and a tap that skipped a celebration land on the next board (an arrow puzzle, 2026-10-17). Decide at press time whether the board takes input (playing, no panel open, no celebration) and drop the tap otherwise. `onClick.Invoke` tests can't see this; drive real pointer events.
- **A Graphic subclass built in code may lack its CanvasRenderer:** `new GameObject("Tap", typeof(RectTransform), typeof(RaycastArea))` (a `MaskableGraphic` used as an invisible hit area) came without one on 6000.3, so every EventSystem raycast threw `MissingComponentException` and all HUD taps would have died on a phone, while an `Image` built the same way was fine. List `typeof(CanvasRenderer)` in the constructor. Only an EventSystem raycast test caught it.
- A raycast-target TMP or Image above a button steals taps; check with an EventSystem raycast, not the rect.
- DOTween safe mode swallows exceptions in callbacks: enable buttons before the tween; in tests set `DOTween.safeModeLogBehaviour = SafeModeLogBehaviour.Error`.
- `TMP_Text.isTextOverflowing` is vertical only: a no-wrap label wider than its box sets nothing; also check `isTextTruncated` and the text bounds.
